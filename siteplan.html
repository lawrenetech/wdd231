Site Name

Nigeria Artifacts Museum

Optional domain: nigeriaartifacts.org

This name was chosen because it directly identifies the site's mission — to preserve, showcase, and educate the public about Nigeria's rich cultural heritage. The word "Artifacts" communicates that the site focuses on physical historical objects (bronzes, terracottas, textiles, masks, and traditional tools), while "Museum" signals a curated, educational experience rather than a marketplace. The name is clear, professional, and easily remembered.

Site Purpose

The site serves as a digital gateway to Nigeria's cultural heritage, offering visitors a place to explore, learn about, and engage with artifacts from across Nigeria's many ethnic groups and historical periods. It provides:

  • Artifact Gallery — a searchable catalog of artifacts with images, descriptions, origin, and historical context.
  • Virtual Tours — curated thematic collections (e.g., "Nok Terracottas," "Benin Bronzes," "Igbo-Ukwu Bronzes") that guide visitors through Nigeria's cultural history.
  • Educational Resources — articles, timelines, and downloadable guides for students, teachers, and researchers.
  • Visit Information — hours, location, ticket pricing, and accessibility details for in-person visits.
  • Contact & Membership — a form for donations, memberships, and inquiries from schools and cultural institutions.

Scenarios

These questions represent typical visitors who will use the site:

  1. "I'm a teacher preparing a lesson on pre-colonial Nigerian history — where can I find images and descriptions of Nok terracotta sculptures?"
    → Answered by the Artifact Gallery and Educational Resources pages.
  2. "I'm visiting Lagos next month — what are the museum's opening hours, ticket prices, and location?"
    → Answered by the Visit page (hours, pricing, address, map).
  3. "How can I donate an artifact or support the museum's preservation work?"
    → Answered by the Contact & Membership page.
  4. "What is the historical significance of the Benin Bronzes and where did they originate?"
    → Answered by the Virtual Tours and individual artifact detail pages.

Color Scheme

The palette uses warm, earthy tones inspired by Nigerian soil, terracotta, and traditional textiles. Deep reddish-brown provides a grounded, museum-like foundation, while warm amber accents echo the colors of ancient bronze and clay.

Typography

One font family is used throughout the site — Inter — with multiple weights to establish hierarchy without introducing inconsistency. Inter's neutral, geometric letterforms let the artifacts themselves be the visual focus while keeping text highly legible.

Inter 700 — Heading 1

Inter 600 — Heading 2 & Section Titles

Inter 400 — Body copy. Used for all paragraph text, form labels, form inputs, list items, artifact descriptions, and general content across the site.

Inter 400 Italic — Captions, image credits, and notes.

Usage Rules

  • Inter 700 (Bold) — Page titles (h1) and hero headings only.
  • Inter 600 (SemiBold) — Section headings (h2), artifact titles (h3), button labels, navigation links.
  • Inter 400 (Regular) — Body text, list items, form inputs, artifact metadata, and footer content.
